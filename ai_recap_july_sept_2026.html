Your AI Catch-Up ☀️

A one-time recap of the AI news you missed — July 1 to September 9, 2026

This is a special one-off edition compiled by a research pass across ~20 sources, cross-checked for accuracy. Confidence is noted for anything not independently corroborated.
🤖 Model Releases
  • OpenAI's GPT-5.6 family (Sol / Terra / Luna) launched July 9, then got a follow-up refresh on Aug 6 that made GPT-5.6 Luna the new free-tier default and gave Plus/Pro users an updated GPT-5.6 Sol with a slider to control reasoning effort, replacing the old Instant/Thinking split. OpenAI classifies the August Sol/Luna release as "High capability" under its Preparedness Framework for both Cybersecurity and Bio/Chemical risk — a notable escalation in its own risk tier. (OpenAI, Aug 6)
  • Anthropic released Claude Opus 5 on July 24 — aimed at complex agentic coding and enterprise work, with a 1M-token context window, 128K max output tokens, and adaptive thinking on by default. (reported by multiple trackers, not independently source-verified)
  • Anthropic followed up with Claude Fable 5.1 and Claude Mythos 5.1 on September 1, marketed as its most advanced models yet for coding and knowledge work. (reported by multiple trackers, not independently source-verified)
  • A "GPT-6 Astra" release on September 4 was mentioned in one lower-reliability tracker — flagging it but not treating it as confirmed; worth double-checking directly with OpenAI's changelog if it matters to you.
💰 Funding & Business
  • Mistral AI raised ~€3B (~$3.5B) in a round led by Samsung Electronics, pushing its post-money valuation past €21B (~$24B) — nearly double its 2025 valuation — as it positions itself as Europe's AI champion. (CNBC, Sept 8)
  • Nvidia's Q2 FY26 revenue hit $96.2B, up 106% year-over-year and beating the $92.2B analyst estimate. Data center revenue alone was $89B ($48.7B of that from hyperscalers). CEO Jensen Huang: "AI has reached its inflection point... compute is revenue, and demand is accelerating." (Fortune, Aug 26)
📜 Policy & Regulation
  • EU AI Act Article 50 transparency rules took effect Aug 2 — AI chatbots must now disclose they're AI, and synthetic content (image/audio/video/text) must be labeled as machine-generated. Non-compliance carries fines up to €15M or 3% of global turnover, whichever is higher. (Al Jazeera, Aug 6)
  • At the same time, the EU's "AI Omnibus" reform postponed the harder high-risk AI system rules — standalone high-risk systems (biometrics, employment, education, essential services, migration) now don't need to comply until Dec 2, 2027, and high-risk AI embedded in regulated products until Aug 2, 2028. It did add one new near-term prohibition: AI systems generating non-consensual sexual content or CSAM become illegal from Dec 2, 2026. (European Commission)
  • China is weighing tighter export/tech controls of its own — reportedly considering banning domestic chip designers from fabricating at TSMC or other foreign foundries, and its Commerce Ministry has discussed limiting Alibaba, ByteDance, and Zhipu from moving training data overseas or letting foreign users download their model weights. It's also weighing tighter rules on foreign acquisitions of Chinese agentic-AI firms. (Tom's Hardware)
  • US chip-export enforcement is porous in practice. Commerce official Jeffrey Kessler told Congress on July 14 that despite ~$10B in approved export licenses for Nvidia H200 chips bound for China, actual shipments have been "trivial." Separately, a loophole let Chinese-owned buyers acquire advanced Nvidia/AMD chips for roughly a year via subsidiaries incorporated in Malaysia, Singapore, and the UAE — since controls were triggered by shipping destination, not ultimate ownership — before Commerce moved to close it. (Tech Times, Jul 15)
⚠️ Controversies & Safety
  • xAI's Grok deepfake scandal escalated further. Grok was responsible for 87% of synthetic files linked to documented deepfake attacks in H1 2026, per Resemble AI's threat report (Aug 12). UK Labour MP Jess Asato sued xAI in June over non-consensual sexualized deepfakes Grok generated of her, and continued to pursue legal action through the summer. (background) · (Tech Times, Aug 13)
